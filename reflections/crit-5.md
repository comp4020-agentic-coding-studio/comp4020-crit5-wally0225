# Crit 5 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was applying what I learned from the previous crit: start
with a clear MVP before expanding scope. I began by focusing only on the core
mechanic — a 5x5 board where the player reads the warning direction, finds a
safe position behind a wall, and avoids the incoming pillars. The initial goal
was just to make that loop playable and understandable without a tutorial.

Once the MVP worked, I could evaluate the experience by actually playing it
and see where it needed to become more engaging. Instead of adding complexity
upfront, I expanded the game based on what the MVP revealed: growing the
board from 5x5 to 7x7, adding background music, and layering in progressive
difficulty — faster pillars, shifting wall positions, and eventually attacks
from multiple directions at once.

This shifted my approach from trying to build the complete product
immediately to building a small, functional foundation first and expanding it
through iteration. The MVP gave me something concrete to evaluate, which made
every later decision about scope and difficulty far more intentional.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a product manager?

This strengthened how I want to approach product development, especially
when working with an AI partner. My role isn't to just ask AI for more
features — it's to plan with AI, establish a clear MVP, evaluate the result,
and decide together what comes next.

Having a working MVP gave me a clear basis for product decisions: was the
experience too easy, too predictable, lacking depth? Those observations
should drive iteration, not a default urge to keep adding features.

More importantly, I see planning with AI as core to my future as a PM.
Rather than treating it purely as an executor of instructions, I want to
treat it as a partner — I bring the product goals, constraints, and user
perspective, and we explore solutions, build the MVP, and iterate together. A
strong PM in an AI-assisted environment needs to be good at deciding what to
build first, why it matters, and when the product is ready to expand.
